SOP 01 · Documentation

Shift Log SOP

Owner: Regional Concierge Manager · Version 1.0 · Source: Apex Shift Log Refresher Training Pack (Part 1); SOP Refresher Module §01; APSF Competency 01
Live

Purpose. Shift logs are the official record of each shift. They provide continuity, accountability, and a professional record for Apex, property management, leasing, and maintenance teams. Logs must be detailed, accurate, and easy to read.

Applies to: every concierge agent, every shift. Site Leads review logs against this SOP.

Quick card · Shift log

  • E.D.O. on every entry: Event, Detail, Outcome.
  • At least 1 time-stamped entry per hour. Quiet hour? Log what you checked.
  • At least 2 site tours per shift, each with route, time and observations.
  • Pass-on every shift, all three sections: Concierge, Maintenance, Leasing.
  • Third person, objective, factual. No opinions, no gossip.
  • Corrections are additions, never deletions.
  • Subject: [Site] — [Date] — [Shift Time] — [Your Name].
  • Submit to the PASS ON LOG email group at end of shift.

1Know what the log is and who reads it

The shift log is not a task. It is the official operational record of the shift. It is read by the next agent, Site Leads and supervisors, property management, leasing, maintenance, HR and Apex leadership — and potentially legal. In the event of an incident, it is a legal document.

Two principles every agent can recite

  • “If someone did not work the shift, they should still fully understand what happened.”
  • “Every shift is marketing.” Management, leasing, and property owners read your log. It reflects Apex's standards — and yours.
An undocumented event did not happen, operationally and legally.

2Write every entry with E.D.O.
E
Event
What happened? One specific, observable sentence. If you can't state it clearly, you don't have an Event yet.
D
Detail
Who, when, where, how? Unit or location, exact time, parties involved. Facts only.
O
Outcome
What did you do? Resolved, escalated or pending. If pending: to whom, and why.

Three questions before every entry

  1. What happened? One sentence.
  2. Do I have the who, where, and when? Unit number, name, exact time — every time.
  3. What did I do about it? Every entry needs an outcome, even if it is “monitoring” or “pending follow-up.”

Liability lives in the Outcome

Every Outcome closes with four parts: the action taken, who was notified by name and channel, the status (resolved, escalated or pending), and the next step with its owner.

Format

  • Time-stamp every entry and tell the story of the shift from beginning to end.
  • Separate entries with spacing. Use bold headers for key incidents.
  • Highlight only the most important pass-off information.

3Meet the minimum requirements every shift
RequirementStandard
FrequencyAt least one time-stamped entry per hour. Gaps mean the agent was not at post, or chose not to document. If nothing occurred, log what you checked: lobby status, desk coverage, cameras.
Site toursAt least two per shift. Each entry has the time, the route and specific observations. “Tour completed” alone is not a log entry.
Resident interactionsComplaints, requests, move-ins and move-outs, deliveries.
IncidentsSecurity, noise, smoking, emergencies. File the incident report as well (SOP 03).
MaintenanceCalls made and requests submitted, with the work-order or service request number.
PackagesDrop-offs, pickups, unit number, expected recipient (SOP 02).
Bottom notesOutstanding items, follow-ups, escalations — carried into the pass-on.

How to tour

  • Tours serve three purposes: security awareness, deterrent presence, community connection.
  • Same route, same observation points: common areas, amenities, entry points, package areas, elevators, stairwells, garage.
  • Note anything unusual, visible maintenance issues, and active policy violations. Greet residents warmly; don't interrogate.

4Complete the pass-on — all three sections

The next agent reads the pass-on before the shift starts. Pass-on notes are required, not optional. All three sections are completed every shift.

SectionWhat goes in it
Concierge Team NotesOutstanding tasks not resolved. Anything the next agent must follow up on. Operational changes or reminders. Package count, perishables and RTS pending.
Maintenance NotesIssue flagged and who was notified. Status: resolved, pending, or escalated. Service request reference if filed.
Leasing NotesResident complaints or requests. Communication gaps. Prospect or new resident interactions.
Strong pass-on

CONCIERGE: 370 packages in system at end of shift. 3 perishables in fridge (Units 421, 817, 1104). RTS pending: 2 (UPS, FedEx) — carrier pickup Friday.

MAINTENANCE: P3 exit door latch broken — does not lock without intentional pressure. Reported to on-site maintenance at 7:45 AM. Service request submitted. Follow-up required.

LEASING: No leasing items this shift.

Weak pass-on

CONCIERGE: Nothing of note.

MAINTENANCE: N/A.

LEASING: N/A.

Test: if your pass-on says “nothing to note” and you had 300+ packages, a perishable in the fridge, and two RTS packages, your pass-on failed.

5Write in a professional, objective voice
  • Third person. “Concierge greeted resident…”, never “I told them…”.
  • Objective and factual. No personal opinions, casual language, emotional language or gossip. Save opinions for private conversations with management.
  • Concise. Every word is attributed to the agent who wrote it.
Strong

9:18 AM — Resident arrived to speak with the property manager. Property manager was notified and assisted the resident.

11:30 AM — Concierge conducted site tour. 3rd floor meeting room occupied by scheduled event. No lease violations observed.

Weak

9:18 AM — helped the resident who came in for the manager. He took care of it.

11:30 AM — did a tour, the meeting is still going on upstairs

6Correct by adding — never by deleting
  • If new information comes in, add a follow-up entry with the new information and the time. Do not delete the original.
  • Update the record — do not duplicate entries.
  • Never edit the record to look better. Correct the behavior going forward.
  • Never copy-paste entries from prior shifts.
Corrections are additions, never deletions.

7Subject line, sign-off and distribution
Subject line[Site] — [Date] — [Shift Time] — [Your Name]
Example: VIA Seaport — 10/08/26 — 7 AM–3 PM — Agent Name
Sign-offSign off at the bottom of the log with your name and shift time.
SubmitAt the end of your shift, to the PASS ON LOG email group.
RecipientsIf “PassOn” does not auto-populate, add recipients manually. CC anyone new if needed and notify leadership.

8Prohibited practices
  • “Nothing happened”, “Quiet night”, “Nothing to report” or “Handled it” logs without documented tours
  • Copy-pasting entries from prior shifts
  • Excessive highlighting, random colors, or cluttered formatting
  • Personal commentary, gossip, or emotional language
  • Leaving the Outcome blank on any entry
  • Writing “N/A” in pass-on sections when anything occurred during the shift
  • Deleting or rewriting an entry instead of adding a correction

9Accountability and how logs are scored
  • Logs are reviewed daily by Apex Leadership and Property Management.
  • Incomplete or unprofessional logs result in coaching and corrective action.
  • Repeated failure to follow standards results in formal discipline.

Shift log scoring rubric

CategoryWeight
E.D.O. Structure & Professionalism15%

Tiers: Strong · Steady · Needs Coaching · At Risk. At Risk carries a written plan with a 30-day checkpoint.

APSF · Documentation Excellence

Under the Apex Professional Standards Framework, logs are scored on five dimensions: E.D.O. Integrity, Outcome Quality, Escalation Judgment, Continuity, and Professional Communication. Level 3 — Apex Standard on all five, plus a passing Witness Test, is the certification bar. There is no averaging.

Level 3 looks like: every entry contains all three E.D.O. components; the one-per-hour minimum is met, including explicit quiet-period entries; both tours are documented with route and specific observations; pass-on complete.

Witness Test — four fatal flaws: open loops with no owner; opinions or blame; escalations done by phone with no written record; timestamps out of order or missing.

10Strong vs. weak examples

Hourly entry

Strong

12:30 PM — Lobby quiet, minimal foot traffic. Desk coverage maintained. Monitoring cameras — no activity.

1:15 PM — Monitoring building cameras. No observable activity. Building remains quiet on all floors.

Weak

12:00 PM — nothing

1:00 PM — (blank)

Site tour

Strong

9:04 PM — Conducted scheduled tour of floors 1–4, pool deck and parking garage. No violations observed. Hallway light out on floor 3 near stairwell exit — submitted maintenance ticket #MT-0817. All entry points secure.

Weak

9 PM — checked building, everything fine.

Incident entry

Strong

3:30 AM — Patio Incident

Event: Escorted residents and guests off patio.

Detail: Group of 4 from Unit 912 playing music. Policy reminder issued.

Outcome: Group complied and left patio. Logged for follow-up.

Weak

“Nothing happened. Saw some people outside.”

Missing detail, context, and follow-up.

The quiet shift

A 4-hour block that reads “Quiet. Nothing to report.” fails the standard. Those four hours required at least two time-stamped entries on lobby status, desk coverage and activity; at least one site tour with route, timestamp and observations; and every package, call or resident interaction, however routine.

A quiet log is written proof that the agent was present and attentive — not that nothing happened.
SOP 02 · Chain of Custody

Package Handling SOP

Owner: Regional Concierge Manager · Version 1.0 · Source: SOP Refresher Module §02; NHE Speed Packet, Module 4
Live

Purpose. Packages are personal property entrusted to Apex's care. Mishandling — storing without logging, misclassifying a high-value item, releasing to the wrong person — creates legal liability, destroys resident trust, and directly threatens the property contract. The standard is zero shortcuts.

Applies to: every agent who receives, stores, releases or returns a package. Leads clear agents on this SOP before solo shifts.

Quick card · Packages

  • Receive → Label → Log → Store → Notify.
  • Log before it moves to storage — in the system and on the package intake sheet.
  • Food delivery: call, refrigerate, dispose after 24 hrs unclaimed.
  • Groceries: daily outreach, 48 hrs, Lead before disposal.
  • High-value: secured storage. Never the general shelf.
  • Outgoing / RTS: no postage, no acceptance.
  • Release: system lookup every time, then read the label against the record.
  • Anything doesn't match? Hold it. Call your Lead.

1Follow the standard process — every package, every time
01
Receive
Confirm recipient name and unit number against current leaseholder records, and check package condition, before anything else.
02
Label
Unit number, date and time received.
03
Log
Enter it in the system (Yardi or your site's package system) and record it on the package intake sheet, before it moves to storage. Never skipped.
04
Store
On the designated shelf or storage for its category. Approved areas only.
05
Notify
Contact the resident through the site's channel now — not at end of shift.
Log it in the system and on the package intake sheet before it goes to storage. No package sits on a shelf unlogged. This is the most commonly skipped step.

2Handle each category by its protocol
CategoryProtocolTimeline & critical distinction
StandardLabel + Log + store on designated shelf + Notify resident.The baseline. No shortcuts on any step.
Perishable — food delivery
DoorDash, UberEats		Label + Log as perishable + call the resident — call, don't just text. No answer in 15–30 minutes: refrigerate. Log the storage and every contact attempt.Dispose after 24 hours if unclaimed, documented. The clock starts at delivery, not at the first contact attempt.
Perishable — groceries
Instacart, AmazonFresh		Label + Log + Refrigerate + daily outreach. Document every contact attempt.48 hours, not 24. If unclaimed at 48 hours, escalate to your Lead before disposal.
High-value
laptops, electronics, luxury goods		Label + Log as high-value immediately + secure in the designated locked or supervised storage + Notify resident promptly. Record the classification in the log entry.Never on the general shelf. Classifying high-value as “standard” or “other” is a prohibited practice.
Outgoing / RTSVerify label and postage + Log + stage for carrier pickup.No postage, no acceptance. Decline politely, explain, and offer alternatives. Do not store it.

Agents confuse these: food delivery is 24 hours; groceries are 48 hours with daily outreach and a Lead before disposal. The fridge is for perishables only.

3Verify before every release

Complete these in order before releasing any package. There are no exceptions based on how well you know the resident.

  1. Run a system lookup to verify the recipient is the resident on file or an authorized guest, before you retrieve the package. Photo ID is not required.
  2. Before handover, read the package label and confirm the name and unit match the resident's system record.
  3. Log the pickup in the system immediately — before the resident leaves.
  4. If anything does not match: hold the package and contact your Lead.
Log it. Hold it. Escalate. Never guess with someone else's property.

Never release on verbal confirmation alone, never skip the label check, and never release under pressure.

4Unmatched recipient

A package arrives with a name that matches no current leaseholder, for a valid unit number. Never guess and never store without verification. Work the sequence and log every step:

  1. Check current leaseholders.
  2. Check the guest list.
  3. Contact the resident of the unit for verification.
  4. If the resident confirms it is not theirs, check past residents.
  5. If you still cannot confirm, process for RTS and escalate to your Lead.

5Missing-package dispute

A resident says a package was delivered and is missing. This is a system-failure scenario. Use A.C.T.

A
Acknowledge
Acknowledge the frustration before doing anything else. Do not get defensive.
C
Control
Lower your voice and gather the facts: unit number, recipient name, carrier, tracking number, delivery date.
T
Transfer or resolve
Begin a package audit now: the system, the package room, and intake logs for that date. Involve your Lead if unresolved.
  • File an incident report (SOP 03).
  • Investigate through intake records, audits and carrier tracking.
  • Do not promise the package will be found. Promise a thorough investigation and a follow-up.
The log is your defense. If the intake was documented correctly, the timeline is clear.

6Reconcile daily and pass it on
  • The package room is reconciled against the system daily.
  • Carry the count into the Concierge section of the pass-on: packages in system at end of shift, perishables in the fridge with unit numbers, and RTS pending with carrier and pickup day.
  • Any discrepancy is logged with E.D.O. and escalated to your Lead.

7Document every package interaction with E.D.O.
Model entry · package discrepancy

3:00 PM — Package Discrepancy

Event: Package for Unit 202 found in lobby — not logged in system, no notification sent.

Detail: Package located in main lobby, no label, no Yardi entry. Carrier unknown.

Outcome: Package logged in Yardi immediately. Labeled with unit and date. Stored in package room. Resident notified.

8Prohibited practices
  • Storing any package without logging it in the system first
  • Moving a package before the system entry is complete
  • Classifying a high-value item as “standard” or “other”
  • Storing packages in unapproved areas (the fridge is for perishables only)
  • Releasing a package without a system lookup, on verbal confirmation alone, or without reading the label against the record
  • Accepting outgoing packages without proper postage
  • Removing any package, mail or supplies from the premises (Handbook: Standards of Conduct)
SOP 03 · Reporting

Incident Reporting SOP

Owner: Regional Concierge Manager · Version 1.0 · Source: Incident Reporting SOP (Revised Draft); NHE Speed Packet, Module 4
Filing method: liveWhen-to-file & chain: pending approval

Purpose. A clear, consistent process for when and how concierge staff document and submit incident reports — so accountability, communication and records are the same across every property.

Applies to: every agent on shift. Site Leads review each submission for accuracy and detail.

Quick card · Incident reports

  • Safety, legal or police? Phone the Regional Manager immediately, then file.
  • File in Connecteam → Forms → Incident Report.
  • Write it with E.D.O. Facts only — what you observed, not what you concluded.
  • Attach photos, stills, system logs or receipts when applicable.
  • Add your site manager emails from the site binder.
  • Submit before the end of the shift it happened in.
  • Log it in the shift log and flag it in the pass-on.
  • When in doubt, file.

1Recognize an incident Pending approval

An incident is any event that disrupts operations, poses a risk to resident safety, damages property, or could lead to escalation with property management. File promptly for:

CategoryExamples
Accidents or injuriesEmployees, residents or guests injured — slips, falls, medical emergencies.
Property damageDamage to building systems, equipment, or resident belongings.
Security breachesUnauthorized access, theft, or violation of security protocols.
Complaints or disputesNoise issues, resident conflicts, or situations requiring management intervention.

Always file for

  • Any physical altercation, threat or confrontation
  • Any injury to a resident, guest or visitor, even minor
  • Any property damage
  • Unauthorized entry, security breach, or suspicious activity acted on
  • Any medical emergency, including EMS response
  • A complaint escalated or unresolved at the desk
When in doubt, file. An unnecessary report is recoverable. A missing one is not.

Leadership will finalize whether additional categories warrant incident reports, to keep every site aligned.

2Critical incidents: phone first

For critical incidents — safety, legal, or police involvement — notify the Regional Manager by phone immediately, then follow up with the written report.

If life or safety is at risk, call 911 first. See SOP 04 — Emergency Response.

3Open the form in Connecteam

Complete the report in Connecteam → Forms → Incident Report immediately after the incident. The fillable form walks you through a step-by-step questionnaire so no details are missed.

4Write it with E.D.O. — facts only
E
Event
What happened? Facts only, no assumptions.
D
Detail
Who was involved, where, when, how?
O
Outcome
Resolution, escalation, or next steps.

Every report includes

  • Exact time — “approximately” if unsure, never invented precision
  • Specific location
  • Full names and roles of those involved
  • What was reported vs. what you saw
  • Who you notified, by name and channel
  • Witnesses — and exactly what they saw
  • How the interaction ended, and what follow-up is open

Write what you observed, not what you concluded

Never writeWrite instead
“The resident was drunk.”“Resident exhibited slurred speech and unsteady gait.”
“He was being aggressive.”“Subject moved toward the desk with a raised voice.”
“The elevator closed on her leg.”“The resident reported that an elevator door closed on her leg.”
“She had serious bruising.”“Concierge observed a small visible abrasion on the resident's knee.”
“The situation was handled.”“Supervisor notified at 9:42 PM. Resolved by 9:55 PM.”
  • Giving medication, diagnosing, or judging severity
  • Bias, personal commentary, or assumptions
  • Editing the record to look better — correct the behavior going forward

5Attach the evidence

Attach photos, camera stills, system logs, or receipts when applicable.

Security footage: never photograph, record or distribute security camera footage on a personal device (Handbook: Photographing, Taping or Recording at Work). If a camera still is needed for the report, ask your Site Lead how it is pulled at your site.

6Add your site manager emails

When you submit the form, add your site manager emails listed in the site binder so local management has visibility. Reports are automatically stored in Connecteam and shared with Management.

7Submit before the end of your shift
  • Reports are submitted before the end of the shift in which the incident occurred.
  • Log the incident in the shift log with E.D.O. and note that the report was filed.
  • Flag any open follow-up in the pass-on, with its owner.

8Know the review chain Pending approval
  • Agent / Frontline Staff — completes the Incident Report form in Connecteam immediately after the incident.
  • Site Lead / Supervisor — reviews the submission for accuracy and detail; follows up with the agent if clarification is needed.
  • Regional Manager — escalates to Property Management if necessary and flags Executive Leadership if the situation is serious.
  • Executive Leadership — final review for high-level escalations, legal considerations, or repeated issues.

9Why incident reports matter
DocumentationAn official record that protects employees and the company.
Analysis & preventionIdentifies trends and helps prevent repeat issues.
CommunicationManagement and leadership are informed quickly.
AccountabilityReinforces professionalism and responsibility among staff.
SOP 04 · Escalation & Safety

Emergency Response SOP

Owner: Regional Concierge Manager · Version 1.0 · Source: Escalation & Emergency Interactive Training; APEX 2026 Employee Handbook (Emergency Evacuation); NHE Speed Packet, Module 4
Live

Purpose. The same first actions, from every agent, in every emergency. You are not a first responder. You are the organized, professional point of contact who gets the right people the right information at the right time.

Applies to: every agent on shift. These protocols must be immediate — not recalled.

Quick card · Emergencies

  • Call first. Document everything. Support within your role.
  • Ask: Can I resolve this within my role and authority? Yes: resolve and log. No: escalate and log.
  • Fire: don't investigate · 911 · alarm · exits, no elevators · no re-entry until all-clear.
  • Medical: 911 · don't move them · stay with them · meet EMS.
  • Security threat: don't confront · keep distance · 911 if credible · supervisor.
  • Your safety comes first. Remove yourself from danger.
  • Critical incident: phone the Regional Manager immediately, then file.
  • Name who you notified, when, and through what channel.

1Know your role: the Apex rule
“Call first. Document everything. Support within your role.”

Do not investigate, do not confront, do not treat. Call the professionals, keep people safe within your authority, and create the record.

Your job is
  • Call 911 first when life or safety is at risk
  • Notify your supervisor and the correct escalation tier
  • Keep residents and yourself safe within your role
  • Observe and document — times, actions, who you notified
  • Guide responders and residents calmly
Your job is not
  • Investigating a fire or a suspicious package yourself
  • Confronting or physically intervening in a threat
  • Providing medical treatment or moving an injured person
  • Deciding a serious event “isn't worth” reporting
  • Handling it verbally and leaving no record

2Decide: resolve or escalate

Before you act, ask the one question that governs every escalation at Apex:

“Can I resolve this within my role and authority?”
Yes — resolve
  • Take action within your role scope
  • Log the event using E.D.O.
  • Note any follow-up required in the pass-on
No — escalate
  • Contact your supervisor immediately
  • Give: what happened, who is involved, what you've done so far
  • Log the escalation — time and who was notified

Escalation is strength. The agent who escalates correctly protects themselves, the resident, and the company.

3Always escalate and document

No judgment call. These are always escalated and documented:

  • Any injury, damage, or physical altercation
  • Any security concern or suspicious person acted upon
  • A resident complaint escalated or unresolved at desk level
  • A policy gray area with no clear answer — escalate before acting

4Fire
Fire protocol
  1. Do not investigate the source. Call 911 immediately, then your supervisor.
  2. Pull the fire alarm if it is not already sounding.
  3. Direct residents to the nearest exit. No elevators.
  4. Go to the designated assembly point. Do not re-enter.
  5. Meet emergency responders at the entrance.
  6. No re-entry until the fire department gives the all-clear.
  7. Document: alarm time, your actions, who you notified.

5Medical
Medical protocol
  1. Call 911. Give the address, floor, unit, and what is happening.
  2. Do not move the person unless they are in immediate danger.
  3. Stay with them — calm and professional. Never give medication or diagnose.
  4. Clear bystanders and keep a path open for EMS.
  5. Meet EMS at the entry point. Provide access and information.
  6. Document: time, what was observed, who responded, outcome. Every medical emergency, including EMS response, gets an incident report (SOP 03).

6Security threat
Security threat protocol
  1. Do not confront. Keep your distance and a calm demeanor.
  2. Call 911 if there is a credible threat to safety.
  3. Notify your supervisor — by radio or a second line.
  4. Observe and document: description, direction of travel, what was said.
  5. Follow law enforcement direction.
  6. Your safety is not secondary to the building's. Remove yourself from danger first.

The step agents miss most: never try to de-escalate an active physical threat yourself. Call 911, do not confront, do not intervene. Your job is communication and documentation.

7Evacuate

From the APEX 2026 Employee Handbook, Emergency Evacuation:

  1. In a fire, activate the alarm system by pulling a fire alarm. Do not investigate the source of a potential fire or hazardous-material emergency.
  2. Report any suspected emergency immediately. Reporting is the first essential step to protecting yourself and others.
  3. When the alarm sounds, evacuate in an orderly manner through the nearest exit.
  4. Do not use elevators. Descend stairwells in an orderly manner.
  5. Go to the designated meeting location away from the building exits.
  6. Report immediately to the supervisor on duty or a member of the Apex Management Team. Stay there, if safe, until you are accounted for and authorized to leave.
  7. No re-entry until an official all-clear is given.

Know your site: confirm the assembly point, AED and fire panel locations with your Lead on your site walk. If you might need assistance to evacuate, tell Apex management so it can be planned.

8Notify the right tier — and record it

Escalating isn't just “telling someone.” The tier must be right, the notification must be named, and it must appear in the log: who, when, through what channel, and what you were told to do. An undocumented escalation did not happen.

Weak

“Notified management about the incident.”

Which manager? Called or messaged? Before or after the resident left?

Apex standard

“Contacted Lead Concierge M. Torres by phone at 11:42 PM per Tier 2. Instructed to complete incident report and monitor. PM to be briefed at 8 AM — flagged in pass-on.”

  • Safety, legal or police involvement: call the Regional Manager by phone immediately, then submit the written report.
  • File in Connecteam → Forms → Incident Report before the end of your shift — always.
  • Add your site manager emails (from the site binder) on submission.
When in doubt — file, and escalate up a tier.

9Use A.C.T. with an upset resident
A
Acknowledge
Recognize what's happening. Don't react — respond. Let them know they've been heard first.
C
Control
Control the environment, then the conversation. Lower your voice. Create space. Don't match their energy.
T
Transfer or resolve
Resolve within your authority, or escalate to the right person. Document either way.
“If someone reviewed this incident tomorrow, would they know exactly what happened, who I called, and when?”
