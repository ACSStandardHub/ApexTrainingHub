1 · Identity & presence

Who Apex is, what you represent, and how the standard looks, sounds and feels on shift.

You are not just a concierge. You are Apex.

Every interaction is a brand moment. Residents don't see the company, they see you. This role carries reputational weight for the property and for Apex.

The H.O.M.E. values
H

Hospitality

We create experiences, not transactions.

Greet by name. Hold the elevator. Offer help before they ask.

Missing looks like: head down, phone in hand, a resident walks past feeling invisible.

O

Ownership

We don't blame. We solve.

A package is missing: you investigate, document, follow up and close the loop.

Missing looks like: passing problems to the next shift.

M

Mindfulness

We stay present. We anticipate.

Notice the resident who looks stressed. Remember the package that was coming.

Missing looks like: reactive service; residents feel like a number.

E

Excellence

We don't fluctuate based on mood.

Monday morning and Friday night get the same standard, watched or not.

Missing looks like: great one day, forgettable the next.

The Apex Standard: five non-negotiables
StandardWhat it means
ConsistencyThe same professionalism at 8 AM, at midnight, and on a slow Tuesday.
IntentionalityNothing about how you present, communicate or operate is an accident.
OwnershipYou don't wait to be corrected. You hold the standard because it is yours.
RepresentationYou are Apex on shift, and near the property off shift.
Non-negotiableThe standard doesn't drop for volume, resident behavior, or how your day is going.

The real test: what does the standard look like at 4:45 AM on a quiet overnight with no residents in sight? The standard is not for an audience.

Proactive, not reactive
  • Luxury is the quality of experience you deliver, not the expensive building.
  • Reactive service waits for the resident to name a problem. Proactive service prevents the ask.
  • The resident who is difficult today gets the same service as the one who is pleasant.
  • Emotional intelligence is a core job skill. It's how you manage the lobby under pressure.
Appearance: the uniform standard

Front desk concierge

  • Black suit jacket, black pants (or below-the-knee skirt), clean and wrinkle-free
  • White button-down shirt or blouse, pressed
  • Tie with the jacket (provided), clean and properly knotted
  • Black, clean, closed-toe professional shoes. No sneakers or sandals
  • Name tag on the upper left chest, always

Package room concierge

  • Black button-down collared shirt, pressed, tucked in preferred
  • Black professional pants. No jeans or yoga pants
  • Black, clean, closed-toe dress shoes. No laced sneakers or sandals
  • Name tag on the upper left chest, always

Grooming, hair and fragrance stay understated. Facial piercings covered or removed except minimal earrings. Be in full uniform at your post at your start time; changing on site is not paid time. Hair and facial hair consistent with cultural, ethnic or racial identity are always permitted, and accommodations are available through your supervisor.

Energy & desk
  • You set the temperature of the lobby. Tired or distracted energy is visible, and contagious.
  • Posture, eye contact and body language speak before you do. Make eye contact within 5 seconds of an approach.
  • No food, bags or face-up phone at the desk. The desk shows readiness, not personal comfort.
  • Handle personal matters before your shift. The lobby is not the place.
What you say
Instead of thisSay this
"I don't know.""Let me find that out for you right now."
"That's not my job.""Let me connect you with the right person."
"They haven't come yet.""I'll follow up on that and get back to you."
"I can't help with that.""Here is what I can do for you."
"There's nothing I can do.""Here is who I'm connecting you with, and why."

Say each one out loud. They need to come out automatically under pressure.

Friendly is required. Familiar is a liability.

Friendly, required

  • Warm greeting by name
  • Genuine interest in resident wellbeing
  • Proactive offers of help
  • The same warmth for every resident, every time

Familiar, never

  • Nicknames, casual address, favoritism
  • Personal conversations beyond the role
  • Opinions on internal matters or other residents
  • Special treatment based on relationship
The test: "Would I behave the same way with every resident, or only this one?" If only this one, you've crossed into familiar. Friendly is consistent. Familiar is selective.

Who you serve, who you report to, and the attendance, coverage and conduct rules. No gaps and no surprises at your 30-day review.

You report to

Apex management and your site supervisor or Lead. All direction flows through them.

You support

Residents first, then property management, leasing and maintenance. Supporting someone doesn't mean following their orders outside your role.

"They said it was approved" is not authorization. If someone you can't verify asks for unit access, confirm it yourself with your supervisor or property management first. If it's confirmed, log who asked, who you verified with, what they said, and the time.
Attendance & call-out
  • Your first 90 days are the performance window. Tardiness and absences show up in your 30- and 90-day reviews.
  • Call out at least 2–4 hours before your shift. Apex expects as early as possible.
  • Call AND text your supervisor and the on-call manager. Give a reason and your expected return. Call in each day you'll be out.
  • Out more than 2 consecutive days: return with documentation.
  • No more than 3 unexcused absences in any 90-day period.
  • Missing a shift with no call and no show is treated as a voluntary resignation unless extenuating circumstances apply.
  • If you don't follow the procedure, the absence is unexcused, whatever the reason. Protected leave (sick time, jury duty, FMLA/PFML and others) never counts against you.

Time, breaks & sick time
  • Clock in and out in Connecteam, accurately, every shift. Never work off the clock, and never clock for someone else.
  • 6+ consecutive hours earns a 30-minute paid meal break. If you're the only staff on site, you stay on the premises.
  • The desk is never left unattended. Hand off keys and responsibilities before you step away.
  • Massachusetts earned sick time accrues at 1 hour per 30 worked, usable after your first 90 days. Give notice in the scheduling app and to your supervisor.
Conduct, devices & confidentiality

Devices

  • Personal phone use only on breaks and meal periods
  • Silenced and stored at the desk: not face-up, not visible
  • Personal calls on shift for emergencies only
  • No earbuds or headphones on post

No fraternization

  • No romantic or personal relationships with residents
  • No exchanging personal contact info or socializing outside work
  • No financial arrangements or recruiting residents for outside services
  • Small holiday tokens are fine. Any personal item (furniture, valuables) must be documented in writing to management

Confidentiality

  • Resident names, units, complaints and personal matters stay private
  • No photos, video or recordings of residents, common areas or incidents
  • No social media about the property, residents or anything seen on shift
  • It doesn't expire when your employment ends

Respect

  • Zero tolerance for harassment of any kind, toward anyone
  • Report what you experience or witness to your supervisor or management
  • Retaliation against anyone who reports is itself a terminable offense
  • Coworker conflicts go privately to your supervisor, never at the desk

3 · Desk operations & the shift log

The operational non-negotiables of every shift, and the E.D.O. method. This is the most important hour of the day.

Your shift begins before you sit down
  1. Arrive 10 minutes early. Never clock in and take the desk unprepared.
  2. Read the pass-on log in full before the outgoing agent leaves.
  3. Confirm open items: pending packages, active maintenance, known resident concerns.
  4. Check the desk: log open, radio charged, call sheet current, supplies stocked.

The desk is a post, not a seat. It is never unattended.

  • Secure relief before stepping away for a bathroom or break.
  • If relief is unavailable or late, notify your supervisor and stay.
  • Abandoning your post without coverage is a terminable offense.
Site tours
  • Minimum 2 per shift, each logged with E.D.O.
  • Three purposes: security awareness, deterrent presence, community connection.
  • Same route, same observation points: common areas, amenities, entry points, package areas, elevators, stairwells, garage.
  • Note anything unusual, visible maintenance issues, and active policy violations. Greet residents warmly; don't interrogate.
What the shift log is

A time-stamped professional record, read by the next agent, supervisors, property management, HR, and potentially legal. The next agent should be fully briefed from your log alone.

An undocumented event did not happen, operationally and legally.

The E.D.O. method · every entry, every time
E

Event

What happened? One specific, observable sentence. If you can't state it clearly, you don't have an Event yet.

D

Detail

Who, when, where, how? Unit or location, exact time, parties involved. Facts only.

O

Outcome

What did you do? Resolved, escalated or pending. If pending: to whom, and why.

Liability lives in the Outcome. Every closing has four parts: the action taken, who was notified by name and channel, the status (resolved, escalated or pending), and the next step with its owner.
Strong vs. weak

Weak tour entry

9 PM — checked building, everything fine.

Strong tour entry

9:04 PM — Conducted scheduled tour of floors 1–4, pool deck and parking garage. No violations observed. Hallway light out on floor 3 near stairwell exit — submitted maintenance ticket #MT-0817. All entry points secure.

Weak pass-on

CONCIERGE: Nothing of note. MAINTENANCE: N/A. LEASING: N/A.

Strong pass-on

CONCIERGE: 370 packages in system. 3 perishables in fridge (units 421, 817, 1104). RTS pending: 2 (UPS, FedEx), carrier pickup Friday. MAINTENANCE: P3 exit door latch broken — reported to A. Mejia 7:45 AM, service request submitted. Follow-up required.

Practice: write three entries

Write each one before opening the model. Your trainer reviews these with you in the coach block. Use realistic times.

A · Propped door

You see a resident's guest propping the main lobby door open with a chair while moving boxes. You address them. The guest complies and removes the chair.

2:12 PM — Lobby Door Propped E: Main lobby entry door observed propped open with a chair by a resident's guest moving boxes. D: Guest (name not provided) moving boxes for Unit [###]. Concierge advised guest that entry doors must remain closed for building security. O: Guest removed the chair at 2:14 PM; door confirmed closed and latched. No further action required.

B · Hallway flood

Unit 614 calls to report the 6th-floor hallway is flooded. Water appears to be coming from under the door of Unit 612. You contact on-call maintenance.

9:20 PM — Water in Floor 6 Hallway E: Resident of Unit 614 reported water flooding the 6th-floor hallway, appearing to come from under the door of Unit 612. D: Call received 9:20 PM. Concierge confirmed standing water outside Unit 612 at 9:24 PM. Unit 612 called — no answer. O: On-call maintenance ([name]) contacted by phone 9:25 PM, ETA [time]. Supervisor notified by phone 9:27 PM. Incident report filed. Pending maintenance — flagged for pass-on.

C · Garage entry

An unknown person enters through the parking garage door. They say they're visiting a friend but can't give a unit number or name. You ask them to leave and they comply.

1:05 AM — Unverified Person, Garage Entry E: Unidentified individual entered the building through the P1 garage door. D: Individual stated they were visiting a friend but could not provide a resident name or unit number. Description: [height, clothing, direction of travel]. O: Individual asked to leave; exited via garage door at 1:09 AM. Supervisor notified by phone 1:10 AM. Garage door checked and secured. Flagged for pass-on.

Release

Verify identity by photo ID or system lookup before you retrieve the package, every time, however well you know them. If anything doesn't match, hold it and call your Lead. Never release under pressure.

Never

  • Store a package unlogged
  • Classify high-value as standard or "other"
  • Release on verbal confirmation
  • Accept outgoing without postage
  • Store in unapproved areas (fridge is for perishables only)

Package room vs. system is reconciled daily. A potentially missing package is investigated through intake records, audits and carrier tracking. Name not on the lease? Check the guest list, contact the unit, check past residents, then RTS and escalate to a Lead.

Incident reports
When-to-file & chain: pending approval

Always file for

  • Any physical altercation, threat or confrontation
  • Any injury to a resident, guest or visitor, even minor
  • Any property damage
  • Unauthorized entry, security breach, or suspicious activity acted on
  • Any medical emergency, including EMS response
  • A complaint escalated or unresolved at the desk

When in doubt, file. An unnecessary report is recoverable. A missing one is not.

How to file

  • Connecteam → Forms → Incident Report
  • Submit before the end of the shift it happened in
  • Add site manager emails so local management sees it
  • Safety, legal or police involvement: phone the Regional Manager immediately, then file

Review chain: Agent → Site Lead/Supervisor → Regional Manager → Property Management / Executive Leadership.

Write what you observed, not what you concluded
Never writeWrite instead
"The resident was drunk.""Resident exhibited slurred speech and unsteady gait."
"He was being aggressive.""Subject moved toward the desk with a raised voice."
"The elevator closed on her leg.""The resident reported that an elevator door closed on her leg."
"She had serious bruising.""Concierge observed a small visible abrasion on the resident's knee."
"The situation was handled.""Supervisor notified at 9:42 PM. Resolved by 9:55 PM."

Every report needs: exact time ("approximately" if unsure, never invented precision), specific location, full names and roles, what was reported vs. what you saw, who you notified by name and channel, witnesses (say exactly what they saw), how the interaction ended, and what follow-up is open. Never give medication, diagnose, or judge severity. Never edit the record to look better; correct the behavior going forward.

Emergencies · call first, document everything, support within your role

You are not a first responder. You're the organized point of contact who gets the right people the right information. Before any escalation, ask: Can I resolve this within my role and authority? Yes: resolve and log. No: escalate and log.

Fire

  1. Don't investigate. Call 911, then your supervisor.
  2. Pull the alarm if it isn't already sounding.
  3. Direct residents to exits. No elevators.
  4. Meet responders at the entrance.
  5. No re-entry until the fire department clears it.
  6. Document alarm time, actions, notifications.

Medical

  1. Call 911: address, floor, unit, what's happening.
  2. Don't move the person unless there's immediate danger.
  3. Stay with them, calm and professional.
  4. Clear bystanders; keep a path for EMS.
  5. Meet EMS at the entry point.
  6. Document time, observations, responders, outcome.

Security threat

  1. Don't confront. Keep distance.
  2. Call 911 if the threat is credible.
  3. Notify your supervisor by radio or second line.
  4. Observe and document: description, direction, words.
  5. Follow law enforcement direction.
  6. Your safety comes first. Remove yourself from danger.
