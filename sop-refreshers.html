Before You Begin
The operational backbone of every property
Shift logs, packages, incidents. Not supporting skills.
Shift log documentation, package handling and incident reporting are not supporting skills. They are the operational backbone of every Apex property. An agent can have excellent presence, warmth and professionalism and still create liability, erode trust and compromise the contract through poor documentation or careless package management. The standard here is not negotiable, and neither is your role in enforcing it.

Use these refreshers with any agent at any point. Each one tells you what the standard is, shows what right and wrong look like side by side, and gives you scenario drills to run on a live shift. Training does not end at sign-off. The agent should never just read this. They should demonstrate it. Regional Concierge Manager

How to use these refreshers

01

Run as a Drill

Do not lecture. Pull up a scenario, read it aloud, and have the agent respond out loud or in writing. Then debrief against the Apex Answer.

02

Use the Live Shift

The best coaching happens in the moment. If a package comes in during the drill, pause, process it correctly together, then debrief. If a log entry is due, write it together and score it.

03

Sign Off Before Solo

Do not clear an agent for solo shifts until they can demonstrate every readiness item without prompting. The Ready Looks Like checklists are your clearance gate.

Refresher 01
Shift Log Mastery
E.D.O. Method · Non-Negotiables · Pass-On Standards
Drills revealed0 / 5

Why this matters

The shift log is not a task. It is the official operational record of the shift, submitted to property management, leasing, maintenance and Apex leadership. It is read by people who were not on the shift. It is a legal document in the event of an incident. It is how management evaluates judgment, professionalism and presence. A log that is vague, incomplete or unprofessional is not a minor issue. It is a performance failure.

Two principles every agent must be able to recite
"If someone did not work the shift, they should still fully understand what happened."
"Every shift is marketing."
Management, leasing and property owners read your log. It reflects Apex's standards, and yours.

The E.D.O. Method

Every entry. Every time. Non-negotiable.

E
Event
What happened? State it clearly in one sentence. If you can't, you don't have an Event yet.
D
Detail
Who was involved? What unit or location? Exact time? Specific circumstances?
O
Outcome
What did you do? Resolved, escalated or pending? If pending: to whom, and why?
Three questions before every entry
  1. What happened? One sentence. If you can't state it clearly, you don't have an Event yet.
  2. Do I have the who, where and when? Unit number, name, exact time. Every time.
  3. What did I do about it? Every entry needs an outcome, even if it's "monitoring" or "pending follow-up."

The four non-negotiables

1Entry Consistency — minimum 1 entry per hour

Gaps indicate one of two things: the agent was not at their post, or nothing happened and they chose not to document it. Neither is acceptable. If nothing occurred, log that explicitly.

✓ Strong
12:30 PM — Lobby quiet, minimal foot traffic. Desk coverage maintained. Monitoring cameras — no activity. 1:15 PM — Monitoring building cameras. No observable activity. Building remains quiet on all floors.
✕ Weak
12:00 PM — nothing 1:00 PM — (blank)
2Site Tours — minimum 2 per shift, with outcomes

Every tour must include a timestamp and what was specifically observed. "Tour completed" alone is not a log entry. Tours serve three purposes: visibility, safety and service presence.

✓ Strong
7:45 AM — Site tour completed. P2/P3 garage & pet wash clear & secured. Exit doors not fully locking without intentional pressure (see Maintenance Notes). Gym & common areas in working order.
✕ Weak
7:30 AM — site tour conducted tour — nothing to report
3Pass-On Notes — required, not optional

The next agent reads pass-on notes before the shift starts. If your pass-on says "nothing to note" and you had 300+ packages, a perishable in the fridge and two RTS packages, your pass-on failed. All three sections must be completed every shift.

Concierge Team NotesOutstanding tasks not resolved. Anything the next agent must follow up on. Operational changes or reminders.
Maintenance NotesIssue flagged and who was notified. Status: resolved, pending or escalated. Service request reference if filed.
Leasing NotesResident complaints or requests. Communication gaps. Prospect or new resident interactions.
✓ Strong pass-on
CONCIERGE: 370 packages in system at end of shift. 3 perishables in fridge (units 421, 817, 1104). RTS pending: 2 (UPS, FedEx) — both need carrier pickup Friday. MAINTENANCE: P3 exit door latch broken — does not lock without intentional pressure. Reported to the maintenance supervisor at 7:45 AM. Service request submitted. Follow-up required.
✕ Weak pass-on
CONCIERGE: Nothing of note. MAINTENANCE: N/A
4Professional Tone — objective, third-person, factual

No first-person voice. No personal opinions. No casual language. No gossip. The log is a legal document and a performance record. Every word is attributed to the agent who wrote it.

✓ Strong
9:18 AM — Resident of Unit 146 arrived to speak with the property manager. Property manager was notified and assisted the resident. 11:30 AM — Concierge conducted site tour. 3rd floor meeting room occupied by scheduled event. No lease violations observed.
✕ Weak
9:18 AM — helped the resident who came in for the manager. He took care of it. 11:30 AM — did a tour, the meeting is still going on upstairs

Prohibited practices — name these out loud

  • "Nothing happened" logs without documented tours
  • Copy-pasting entries from prior shifts
  • Excessive highlighting, random colors or cluttered formatting
  • Personal commentary, gossip or emotional language
  • Leaving the Outcome field blank on any entry
  • Writing "N/A" in pass-on sections when anything occurred during the shift

Scenario drills

Read aloud. Agent answers with Event, Detail and Outcome. Do not show the answer first.

Shift log scoring rubric

Every log is scored across seven categories. Share this rubric with the agent. They should know exactly how they are evaluated.

CategoryWeight
The four tiers
Strong

Consistent E.D.O. every hour. Tours completed with outcomes. All incidents escalated and documented. Pass-on notes are actionable. Log stands alone.

Steady

Most entries time-stamped and complete. Minor gaps in pass-on or tour notes. No pattern of missed escalations. Coaching on one or two areas.

Needs Coaching

Entries lack outcomes. Tours missing or undocumented. Pass-on vague or blank. Incidents logged without follow-up. Active coaching plan in place.

At Risk

Multiple non-negotiables missed consistently. Blank sections in live logs. No escalation on safety or maintenance items. Written plan with 30-day checkpoint.

Ready looks like — Shift Log

Coaching Prompt
Pull up the agent's most recent log together. Read one entry aloud and ask: "If you were the incoming agent, what questions would you still have after reading this?" Whatever they name, those are the gaps. Rewrite it before the shift closes.

Package Handling
Intake Protocol · Categories · Verification · Escalation
Drills revealed0 / 6
Package protocol: Pending approvalTeach it as written; it may be updated once leadership signs off.

Why this matters

Packages are personal property entrusted to Apex's care. Mishandling — storing without logging, misclassifying a high-value item, releasing to the wrong person — creates legal liability, destroys resident trust and directly threatens the property contract. Every package that enters the building is a test of the agent's reliability. The standard here is zero shortcuts.

The standard process — every package, every time

01
Receive
02
Log
03
Label
04
Store
05
Notify
  • Receive: confirm recipient name, unit number and package condition before touching anything else.
  • Log in the system before moving the package anywhere. System entry is not the last step. It is the second step. This is the most commonly skipped step.
  • Notify the resident through the site's channel now, not at end of shift.

Package categories & handling

TypeProtocolCoach Note
StandardLog in system + Label + Store on designated shelf + Notify resident.The baseline. No shortcuts on any step.
Perishable — food deliveryLog + Label + Refrigerate + Call resident. Dispose after 24 hours if unclaimed.Call, do not just text. A food delivery sitting unrefrigerated is a liability.
Perishable — groceriesLog + Label + Refrigerate + Daily outreach. Escalate to a Lead and dispose after 48 hours.48-hour window, not 24. Different protocol from food delivery. Agents confuse these.
High-valueLog immediately as high-value + Label + Secure storage (not general shelf) + Notify promptly.Laptops, electronics, luxury goods. Never on the general shelf. Misclassifying as "standard" is a prohibited practice.
Outgoing / RTSVerify label and postage. Log. Stage for carrier pickup. Do not accept outgoing without postage.If a resident drops off without postage, decline politely and explain. Do not store it.

Pickup verification — every handoff

Before releasing any package, complete these steps in order. There are no exceptions based on how well you know the resident.

  1. Verify the recipient is the resident on file or an authorized guest — by photo ID or system lookup. Never assumed, never on verbal confirmation alone.
  2. Confirm the unit number.
  3. Log the pickup in the system immediately, before the resident leaves.
  4. If anything does not match: hold the package and contact a Lead.
When in doubt: Log it. Hold it. Escalate. Never guess with someone else's property.

Prohibited practices — name these out loud

  • Storing any package without logging it in the system first
  • Misclassifying high-value items as generic "standard" packages
  • Moving a package before the system entry is complete
  • Storing packages in unapproved areas
  • Releasing a package without verifying the recipient's identity
  • Accepting outgoing packages without proper postage

Scenario drills

Use a real package or the package room if you can. Agent walks every step out loud. Correct in real time.

Ready looks like — Package Handling

Coaching Prompt
Walk to the package room together. Pick up any package and have the agent walk you through what they would do from the moment it arrived. Watch for the sequence — especially when they log it relative to when they move it.

Incident Reporting & Escalation
When to File · The Chain · Critical Incidents · Tier Documentation
Drills revealed0 / 3

Why this matters

An incident report is the official record that protects the agent and the company. It lets management spot trends and prevent repeats, keeps leadership informed quickly, and reinforces professionalism. An undocumented escalation, operationally and legally, did not happen.

Call first. Document everything. Support within your role. Agents are not first responders. Before any escalation, the agent asks: "Can I resolve this within my role and authority?" Yes: resolve and log. No: escalate and log.

When to file

When-to-file categories: Pending approval

An incident is any event that disrupts operations, poses a risk to resident safety, damages property, or could lead to escalation with property management. Always file for:

  • Any physical altercation, threat or confrontation
  • Any injury to a resident, guest or visitor, even minor (slips, falls, medical emergencies)
  • Any property damage — building systems, equipment or resident belongings
  • Unauthorized entry, theft, security breach, or suspicious activity acted on
  • Any medical emergency, including EMS response
  • A complaint or dispute escalated or unresolved at the desk
When in doubt, file. An unnecessary report is recoverable. A missing one is not. Leadership will finalize whether additional categories warrant reports across all sites.

The chain

Reporting chain: Pending approval
  • Agent / Frontline — completes the Incident Report in Connecteam immediately after the incident.
  • Site Lead / Supervisor — reviews the submission for accuracy and detail; follows up with the agent if anything needs clarifying.
  • Regional Manager — escalates to Property Management if necessary; flags Executive Leadership if the situation is serious.
  • Executive Leadership — final review for high-level escalations, legal considerations or repeated issues.

How to file

  • Connecteam → Forms → Incident Report. The step-by-step questionnaire makes sure no detail is missed.
  • Write it in E.D.O. — Event (facts only, no assumptions), Detail (who, where, when, how), Outcome (resolution, escalation or next steps).
  • Attach photos, camera stills, system logs or receipts when applicable.
  • Add your site manager emails listed in the site binder when submitting, so local management has visibility.
  • Submit before the end of the shift in which the incident occurred.

The critical-incident phone rule

Safety, legal or police involvement: phone the Regional Manager immediately, then follow up with the written report. The phone call comes first. The report is still required.

Documenting the tier — weak vs. Apex

Escalating is not just "telling someone." The log must name who was notified, when, through what channel, and what instruction was received.

Weak"Notified management about the incident." Which manager? Called or messaged? Before or after the resident left?
Apex"Contacted Lead Concierge M. Torres by phone at 11:42 PM per Tier 2. Instructed to complete incident report and monitor. PM to be briefed at 8 AM — flagged in pass-on."

Write what you observed, not what you concluded

Never writeWrite instead
"The resident was drunk.""Resident exhibited slurred speech and unsteady gait."
"He was being aggressive.""Subject moved toward the desk with a raised voice."
"She had serious bruising.""Concierge observed a small visible abrasion on the resident's knee."
"The situation was handled.""Supervisor notified at 9:42 PM. Resolved by 9:55 PM."

Scenario drills

Agent states the call first, then the E.D.O. entry. Every answer ends with "and I logged it."

Ready looks like — Incidents & Escalation

Coaching Prompt
Pick any incident from the last month at this property. Ask the agent: "If this were reviewed tomorrow, would the record show exactly what happened, who you called, and when?" Have them rewrite the escalation line until the answer is yes.
